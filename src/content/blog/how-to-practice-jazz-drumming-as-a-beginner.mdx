"Jazz" is a very broad umbrella term. This article deals with bebop, as an understanding of the content and phrasing developed by the great bop drummers provides valuable insight into all later jazz styles. An aspiring bop drummer should hope to become a strong soloist, and a flexible accompanist. In the community, accompaniment is generally referred to as "comping". 

In order to comp in a creative and compelling fashion, a drummer must develop a great deal of four-way coordination, and a clear mental model of bop phrasing. You can check out these [progressive drumset coordination exercises](https://jva-music.com/pdfs/drumset/independence-exercise-pamphlet/) for a free primer on separating the hands and feet. Acquiring this sort of coordinated independence is a long term goal to be undertaken gradually, and is not a prerequisite for experimenting with comping, so we will delve into some conceptual exercises to help you get a sense of how to phrase and develop your ideas without allowing a lack of four-way coordination to get in your way. 

Let's begin with the jazz ride cymbal beat, a classic jazz rhythm where every strong beat is played, and "skip-beats" (upbeat eighth notes) are added on the & of 2 and & of 4. 

![Music notation for the standard jazz ride cymbal beat with skip-beats on the &of2 and &of4](/uploads/blog/01_standard_jazz_ride.jpg "Jazz Ride Cymbal Beat")

We will treat this as our home base, and the basis for the phrasing ideas we develop moving forward. 

We can vary the ride cymbal beat by first taking simple quarter notes, and then dropping in skip-beats as we hear them, not necessarily on the & of 2 and & of 4, but also on the & of 1 and & of 3 if it feels appropriate. 

![Music notation for a ride cymbal phrase that uses skip-beats freely](/uploads/blog/03_ride_cymbal_phrase.jpg "Ride Cymbal Phrase ")
