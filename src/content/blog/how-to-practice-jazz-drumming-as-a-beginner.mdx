"Jazz" is a very broad umbrella term. This article deals with bebop, as an understanding of the content and phrasing developed by the great bop drummers provides valuable insight into all later jazz styles. An aspiring bop drummer should hope to become a strong soloist, and a flexible accompanist. In the community, accompaniment is generally referred to as "comping". 

In order to comp in a creative and compelling fashion, a drummer must develop a great deal of four-way coordination, and a clear mental model of bop phrasing. You can check out these [progressive drumset coordination exercises](https://jva-music.com/pdfs/drumset/independence-exercise-pamphlet/) for a free primer on separating the hands and feet. Acquiring this sort of coordinated independence is a long term goal to be undertaken gradually, and is not a prerequisite for experimenting with comping, so we will delve into some conceptual exercises to help you get a sense of how to phrase and develop your ideas without allowing a lack of four-way coordination to get in your way. 

Let's begin with the jazz ride cymbal beat, a classic jazz rhythm where every strong beat is played, and "skip-beats" (upbeat eighth notes) are added on the & of 2 and & of 4. 

![Music notation for the standard jazz ride cymbal beat with skip-beats on the &of2 and &of4](/uploads/blog/01_standard_jazz_ride.jpg "Jazz Ride Cymbal Beat")

We will treat this as our home base, and the basis for the phrasing ideas we develop moving forward. 

We can vary the ride cymbal beat by first taking quarter notes, and then dropping in skip-beats as we hear them, not necessarily on the & of 2 and & of 4, but also on the & of 1 and & of 3 if it feels appropriate. All examples included from here on are to demonstrate ideas and inspire your creativity, but you should take time to follow these improvisational prompts on your own to see what you come up with. 

![Music notation for quarter notes played on the ride cymbal](/uploads/blog/02_quarters_only.jpg "Quarters Only")

![Music notation for a ride cymbal phrase that uses skip-beats freely](/uploads/blog/03_ride_cymbal_phrase.jpg "Ride Cymbal Phrase")

The snare can be added on the upbeats, and we begin to get a two-voice dialogue happening between the drum and the cymbal. 

![Music notation for a ride cymbal phrase that uses skip-beats freely while adding the snare drum on upbeats](/uploads/blog/04_same_phrase_with_snare.jpg "Ride Cymbal Phrase With Snare")

The bass drum is great for reinforcing accents in the cymbal phrasing. This phrase has lots of anticipations and syncopations. When the bass drum plays with the cymbal, you might try crashing the cymbal lightly with the shoulder of the stick for emphasis.

![Music notation for a ride cymbal phrase that uses the bass drum to reinforce accents](/uploads/blog/05_new_phrase_bass_drum.jpg "Cymbal and Bass Drum Accents")

You can take these ideas and re-orchestrate them by replacing snare drums with bass drums, or vice versa. Let both drums experiment with either role, then blur the lines and try combining the concepts.

![Music notation for a combination study featuring the jazz ride cymbal beat with the snare drum playing mostly upbeats while the bass drum reinforces anticipations and syncopations](/uploads/blog/06_combination_study.jpg "Combination Study")

The hi hat can be added in many different ways. Fixed hi hat on 2&4 is popular and iconic. The hi hat keeping time on all quarter notes is often thought of as an advanced modern jazz concept, but the sooner you begin experimenting with this idea the faster you will be able to pull it into your playing. The hi hat can also be used as an independent comping voice. Try playing all the examples above and replacing either the snare, or the kick with the hi hat. 

Make sure you treat these ideas as **concepts:** prompts to inspire your own experimentations. Sit down with a click, or a walking bass play along and try to phrase using these ideas. Let your ear guide you. Let great recordings of bop drummers guide your ears. 

95% of all jazz gigs you play will be spent comping, so learning to have fun doing it, and to accompany in a dynamic, expressive way will help you to enjoy the music [gary quote]
